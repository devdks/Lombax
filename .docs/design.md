# Design

Design makes the [concept](concept.md) legible as an experience. It decides what the visitor sees first, where their actions belong, what makes a consequence recognizable, and how the piece gets its own visual identity.

## Compose the scene and controls together

1. **Start with the subject’s structure.** Sketch the important places, objects, or relationships at the same time as the action and its readout. Attach a control or measurement to the thing it changes or measures wherever the subject gives it a natural home.
2. **Choose a medium that earns its place.** Use 3D when height, depth, sightlines, or orientation carry information. Use motion when order, direction, or change over time matters. Use stillness or a flat view when it makes comparison easier. Give visitors a simpler view of the same relationship when that serves the task.
3. **Give the first screen one clear job.** Show the subject in a usable starting state, a visible action, and enough feedback to understand the current outcome. Keep comparisons and secondary controls close to the questions they answer. A full-page composition can arrange the scene, controls, and readings as one surface.
4. **Give the entry its own character.** Derive type, color, materials, and spacing from the subject and their jobs in the interface. Let each entry define its own visual treatment. Keep the archive presentation at the vault entrance and give the piece a clear way back.
5. **Sketch the narrow view as a full interaction.** Keep the question, action, and consequence connected when space changes. Decide which labels move, which comparisons reflow, and how the experience reads without continuous motion.

## Catchment

The sloped 3D courtyard shows the roof, paving, garden, and doorway as a connected site. The plan view makes it easier to follow where the two paths meet. Blue marks show the water routes, site letters sit on the candidate placements, and a leader ties the volume reading to the workshop door. The rainfall control sets the scenario, the timeline lets visitors examine the sequence, and the comparison strip holds the sites against the same 30-minute storm.

An early version put the controls and readings in a dark sidebar. Moving the door reading into the scene and giving the comparison strip its own place along the bottom made it easier to connect the number to the door and the options to the site. That placement principle is the reusable lesson. A different subject will suggest different controls, positions, and materials.

## Handoff to UI

Give [UI](ui.md) the wide and narrow reading order, key states, control placement, and what each use of color, geometry, or motion communicates. Include the moments where the visitor should see cause and effect. The design is ready when someone reviewing the sketches can point to the action, find the consequence, and connect the two.
