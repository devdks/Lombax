# Purpose

Purpose is the visitor’s reason to use the entry. It names a situation and a decision, action, or insight the interface can make easier.

## Find the purpose

1. **Start with a situation.** Name the person, what they are trying to do, and the constraints that matter. A specific workshop entrance at risk of receiving runoff gives the visitor something concrete to care about.
2. **Find the obstacle in the familiar interface.** Name what a plan, list, form, or other usual view leaves hard to see, compare, predict, express, or do. Put the missing relationship into words before looking for a clever interaction.
3. **Define a sign of success.** Describe something a visitor could do or explain after one brief use. A good sign is observable in a walkthrough with realistic inputs.
4. **Name one way it could grow.** Identify a real data source, decision, or workflow that would deepen the same purpose. Keep the first entry focused enough to demonstrate the value on its own.

When using a reference, pay attention to why a control matters, what evidence the visitor sees after using it, and how that evidence supports a decision. Carry that insight into the new subject’s own facts and constraints.

## Catchment

Someone planning an improvement to a workshop courtyard needs to decide where one rain garden will keep the most runoff away from the door. A static site plan can show three available spots. It leaves the visitor to work out which water reaches each spot, how quickly the soil takes it up, and what happens when the basin fills.

The useful outcome is that the visitor can choose a site for a given storm and explain why the choice can change with rain intensity. The first study uses a fictional courtyard and one basin size. A real-site version could use surveyed elevations, measured soil infiltration, and local storm profiles to support the same decision.

## Handoff to concept

Give [Concept](concept.md) the visitor’s situation, the obstacle, the observable outcome, and the first entry’s scope. The purpose is ready when someone can name what becomes easier and how they could tell. When the owner supplies a concept, recover this purpose from the supplied premise and flag any assumption that could materially change it.
